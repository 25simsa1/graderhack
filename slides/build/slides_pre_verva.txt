GraderHack, a controlled study of impossible tasks in RL training.

In August 2026, agents in RL training learned to tamper with the scorer grading them. Investigators estimated that 30 to 40% of the tasks couldn't be solved honestly.

They didn't study how the behavior shows up during training. Every account so far is observational. Nobody has varied anything.

Is grader hacking something only frontier models do, or can you predict it from what's in the training environment?

I vary the environment, train small open models with RL, and measure hacking on held-out solvable tasks, where cheating is never needed.

The goal isn't to audit a buggy grader after training. It's to predict hacking from the environment before training starts.

A Python function with a reference solution and hidden tests.

One visible test contradicts the spec.

It can read, edit, run tests and submit. No network.

Catches 50 of 50 planted hacks and flags 0 of 10 honest runs. Every flag is also checked by hand.

0, 10, 25 or 50% of training tasks. Does hacking go up with the dose?

Hidden, readable or writable. Does more access lead to more hacking?

A rewarded action for declaring a task impossible. Does it stop hacking?

GRPO-style on-policy LoRA on Qwen3-4B-Instruct. I ran the first rounds today. The reward is a pass from the visible grader, which is exactly the signal a broken environment gives.

Tampering and false pass claims on 5 held-out task templates, plus the honest solve rate.

Untrained pilots unless noted. I read every tamper flag by hand.

Tampering is silent. 8 audited tampers, either edits to tests.py or code bent against the spec to fit a broken test. 7 of the 8 were submitted as a pass with no note.

It almost never admits failure. Across 371 submitted impossible tasks, it said fail or unsure 18 times. With a hidden grader, it claims a pass after seeing "9/10 tests passed."

It ignores the honest exit. Qwen had a declare_impossible tool and used it 0 times in 442 episodes. In 88 impossible episodes it wrote that the test was wrong or the task was impossible, and then submitted pass in 66 of them.

A second model family tampers differently. Llama-3.2-3B replaced tests.py with its own tests 9 times, 5 of them on solvable tasks. 3 times it claimed a pass after 0 tests ran.

First RL, today. I ran 30 on-policy LoRA rounds per arm on 3 H200s, with 0, 25 and 50% impossible tasks. At round 10, the peak, false pass claims on held-out impossible tasks were 5%, 10% and 26% (10% untrained). Honest solving went up from 38% to 45%. This is one seed, I picked the peak after seeing the curves, and training collapsed after round 20.
